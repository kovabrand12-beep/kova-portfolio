The process

Live in three to five days

Not three to five weeks. Speed is the point: most of what makes a web project drag has nothing to do with building the site, and we have taken all of it out.

  1. 1

    The call Day one, 15 minutes

    Fifteen minutes is enough: what the business does, who it needs to reach, and the one thing the site has to make happen. You leave the call with a fixed price and a launch date, or an honest "we are not the right fit".

  2. 2

    Build Days two and three

    Structure, copy, design and the conversion flow, built in one pass by one person. You get a live preview link on day two, not a mockup, and it updates as we go.

  3. 3

    Your review Day four

    You go through the preview and send everything you want changed in one message. Turned around the same day in almost every case.

  4. 4

    Launch Day five

    We run the twenty-point pre-launch audit covering speed, accessibility, meta tags, forms, spam protection and broken links. Then we point the domain, verify it live, and hand over documentation written for a non-developer.

What we need

What would slow it down

Three to five days assumes three things are true. They almost always are, but it is only fair to say them up front, because these are the only things that push a build past the week.

  • Real photographs. Pictures of your own premises, your own work and your own team. For a local business this is the strongest trust signal there is, and stock photography visibly is not.
  • Final wording for the important pages. We will write and shape it, but the facts have to come from you, and they have to be true. That means services, areas covered, hours and credentials.
  • One decision-maker. Feedback gathered from four people with different opinions takes three times as long and produces a worse site.
After launch

Hosting and maintenance, if you want it

Launching a site is the easy part. Keeping it accurate is the part most businesses quietly stop doing about four months in.

01

Hosting

We put the site on fast hosting, keep the certificate renewed and the domain pointed where it should be, and make sure it stays up. Static files on good hosting is about as close to unbreakable as a website gets.

02

Maintenance

Content changes, new pages, new photographs, updated hours and prices. Send what you want changed and it gets done, rather than sitting in a queue behind a ticket system.

03

Backups and monitoring

Regular backups you can roll back to, and an eye on whether the site is actually up and the forms are actually delivering. Finding out from a customer is too late.

04

Optional, and we mean it

The domain, the hosting account and the source code are in your name from the first day. You can move the site to anyone, at any time, without asking us and without paying to be let go. That is the difference between a service and a lock-in.
